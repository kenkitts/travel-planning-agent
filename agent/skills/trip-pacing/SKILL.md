# Trip pacing

Procedural rules for pacing a multi-day itinerary once you have enough
information (destination, trip length, and any known weather/activity
preferences) to start drafting or revising a day-by-day plan.

## Rules

1. **Cap outdoor activity count per day using that day's forecast.** Before
   assigning outdoor activities to a specific day, check the weather
   forecast for that day (via the weather tool). If the forecast indicates
   rain, high wind, or extreme temperature for a large part of the day,
   limit that day to at most one outdoor activity and prefer indoor or
   flexible alternatives (museums, markets, indoor dining) for the rest of
   the day.

2. **Leave a buffer day after 3+ consecutive high-activity days.** A
   "high-activity" day is one with 3 or more scheduled activities/stops, or
   any single activity requiring more than half a day (e.g. a full-day
   hike or excursion). After 3 consecutive high-activity days, schedule a
   lighter day next: fewer stops, more free/unstructured time, and avoid
   introducing a new full-day activity.

3. **Sequence stops geographically, not just by preference order.** When
   multiple places are planned for the same day, order them to minimize
   backtracking (e.g. group stops in the same neighborhood/area together)
   rather than listing them in the order the traveler mentioned them.

4. **State the pacing reasoning briefly when it changes the plan.** If a
   rule above caused you to move, drop, or lighten an activity, say so in
   one short sentence (e.g. "Moved the hike to Thursday since Wednesday's
   forecast calls for storms") so the traveler understands why the
   itinerary looks the way it does — don't silently reorder things without
   explanation.

## When this skill does not apply

Skip these rules for single-day/short outings, or once the traveler has
explicitly locked in a fixed schedule and is only asking for minor
adjustments unrelated to pacing (e.g. "add a coffee stop on day 2") — don't
re-litigate the whole itinerary's pacing on every small edit.
